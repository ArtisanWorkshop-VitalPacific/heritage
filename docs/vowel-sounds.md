# Vowel sounds

How Heritage Readers says vowels, and the sources behind it.

## The rule

Long and short are different sounds, not different lengths. A short vowel stays short when it is stretched ("ăăă"). A long vowel says its name. The sound is taught first and the letter's name later (McGuffey, First Reader, Suggestions to Teachers: the Phonic Method).

## McGuffey's Phonic Chart (First Eclectic Reader, 1879)

| Kind | Marks and key words |
|---|---|
| Short vowels | ă am, ĕ end, ĭ in, ŏ odd, ŭ up, o͝o look |
| Long vowels | ā ate, ē eve, ī ice, ō ode, ū use, o͞o fool |
| Other vowels | â care, ä arm, ȧ last, ạ all, ẽ err, û burn |
| Diphthongs | oi oy (oil, boy), ou ow (out, now) |
| Aspirates | f fife, h him, k kite, p pipe, s same, t tat, sh she, ch chat, th thick, wh why |
| Subvocals | b bib, d did, g gig, j jug, n nine, m maim, ng hang, v valve, th this, z zine, z azure, r rare, w we, y yet, l lull |
| Substitutes | ạ for ŏ (what), ê for ā (there), ȯ for ŭ (son), ọ for o͞o (to), ụ for o͝o (full), ȳ for ī (fly), ç for s (cite), ġ for j (gem), ş for z (as), gh for f (laugh), qu for kw (quit) |

The Primer and First Reader mark every new word this way: dŏg, frŏg, lŏg and ŏff are short o, like hŏt; hē, shē, gō and bȳ are long.

## Hay and Wingo (Reading with Phonics, 1946)

Short vowels come first. The ladder reads the pink blend with the short vowel (ha, he—, hi, ho, hu), then the word (ham, hen, hit, hop, hug). The dash on "he—" marks a blend, not the word "he".

## In the app

- Lesson words: Mrs. Wren records the word once (ElevenLabs Multilingual v2, with letter timings). The parts are cut from that recording: onset with the vowel, the vowel held (WSOLA, same pitch and vowel), the vowel with the rest, then the word. "top" is "ta, ah, op... top".
- Short vowel sounds: cut from at, pet, it, odd and up, the cleanest of McGuffey's key words.
- In any spoken line, ⟦a⟧ plays the short vowel and ⟦hat⟧ plays the word sounded out. Captions show "a" and "ha, a, at... hat".
- Heart words (the, said, was), single letters and phonograms are said plainly.
- Words with two readings are recorded in a phrase that fixes the lesson reading: "to read", "the wind".
- Never use IPA length marks to hold a vowel: they turn a short vowel into a different one (/ɪː/ is heard as "ee", /ɛː/ as "air").

## Open question

In the American voice, a few short-o words (log, off) come out closer to the vowel in "all" than in "hot". McGuffey marks them ŏ, like hŏt.
